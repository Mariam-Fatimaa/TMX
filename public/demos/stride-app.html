Screens

Built to read mid-stride

Big numbers, high contrast and one-thumb controls. Everything a runner checks at a glance sits in the top half of the screen.

Features

What runners actually use

We interviewed 40 club and solo runners before drawing a single screen. These six features came up in nearly every conversation.

Accurate GPS

Dual-frequency location with smoothing that keeps your route on the path, even between tall buildings.

Plans that adapt

24 plans from first 5K to marathon. Skip a session and Stride reshuffles the week instead of piling it on.

Audio pace cues

A short voice update each kilometre with split, average pace and whether you are on target. Phone stays in your pocket.

Weekly progress

One chart per week, distance and pace side by side. Compare any two weeks without opening a spreadsheet.

Friends challenges

Set a monthly distance goal with your club or group chat. Private by default, no public leaderboards.

Works offline

Runs record without signal and sync when you are back. Battery use averages 6% per hour on recent phones.

Design notes

Dark app, loud numbers

Most runs start before sunrise or after work. A dark interface saves battery on OLED screens and keeps glare down, while one bright volt accent marks what matters right now.

Components

Every touch target is at least 48 px, because gloves and sweaty thumbs are real. Rings, chips and buttons share an 8 pt grid and two corner radii: 12 for controls, 20 for cards.

User flow

From install to first 10K

Onboarding takes under two minutes. We cut it from nine screens to four after testing with new runners.

  1. Tell us your weekPick your running days and how long you usually have.
  2. Choose a goalFirst 5K, a faster 10K or a half. Stride suggests a plan.
  3. Run the sessionTap Start from Today. Audio cues guide each part of the run.
  4. Review the splitsSee pace per km, heart-rate zones and how it compares to last week.
  5. Race dayThe final week tapers off so you start fresh, not tired.
